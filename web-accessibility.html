Why it matters

Accessibility isn't optional. For most businesses, it's a legal requirement.

26%
of U.S. adults have a disability

One in four Americans. An inaccessible site turns away a quarter of your potential audience before they read a single word.

ADA
Title III applies to websites

Federal courts have consistently ruled that business websites are "places of public accommodation." Inaccessible sites have triggered lawsuits against businesses of all sizes.

SEO
Accessibility improves rankings

Alt text, semantic HTML, and logical heading structure overlap almost entirely with Google's on-page SEO best practices. A more accessible site is a better-ranking site.

WCAG conformance levels

We target Level AA for all client work.

WCAG 2.1 defines three levels of conformance. Most legal requirements and industry standards reference Level AA.

Level A
Minimum

The floor. Non-negotiable criteria that, if missing, make a site unusable for some users. Every site should pass Level A.

Our standard
Level AA
Recommended

The standard we target for all client work. Required by most accessibility laws and regulations worldwide, including ADA enforcement guidance.

Level AAA
Enhanced

The highest level, aspirational for most sites. Some criteria are content-specific and not achievable for all page types.

What we test

A full audit covers every layer of the page.

Automated scanners catch about 30% of real issues. The rest require a developer who understands both accessibility and code.

⌨
Keyboard Navigation

Every link, button, and form element reachable and operable without a mouse. Tab order is logical and matches visual layout.

◑
Color Contrast

All text meets WCAG AA contrast ratios: 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text. We test against all backgrounds, including gradients.

◻
Alt Text

Every meaningful image has a descriptive alt attribute. Decorative images are properly hidden from screen readers with empty alt and aria-hidden.

◈
Form Accessibility

All inputs have associated labels. Error messages are clear, specific, and don't rely on color alone. Required fields are explicitly marked.

▣
Focus Indicators

Visible focus styles maintained throughout. No CSS that removes the browser's default outline without providing a better replacement.

≡
Heading Structure

A single H1 per page, logical H2–H6 hierarchy. Headings communicate structure, not just size. Screen readers use them as navigation landmarks.

◎
ARIA & Semantics

Native HTML semantics used first. ARIA attributes added only where native elements fall short, with roles, states, and properties applied correctly.

↔
Zoom & Reflow

Content remains readable and usable at 200% zoom without horizontal scrolling. Text resizes without loss of content or functionality.

How it works

Audit to verified in four steps.

01
Audit

Automated scan plus manual review by a developer. Automated tools catch roughly 30% of issues; the rest require human judgment. We do both.

02
Report

A prioritized list of issues by severity (critical, serious, moderate) with specific fix instructions for each one. Not a generic checklist.

03
Remediate

We fix the issues in code, not just flag them. Every change tested across screen readers (NVDA, VoiceOver) and keyboard-only navigation.

04
Verify

Re-test after remediation and document conformance. Ongoing monitoring available as part of a Care Plan so new content doesn't re-introduce issues.

New builds

Every new COLEwebdev site is built to WCAG 2.1 AA from day one.

Accessibility isn't a post-launch audit for the sites we build; it's a development standard. Semantic HTML, proper contrast, keyboard navigation, and screen reader compatibility are part of our build checklist on every project.

The audit and remediation service is for existing sites built by others, or for clients who need a third-party conformance report for legal or procurement purposes.
